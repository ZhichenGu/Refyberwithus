Understanding the Problem

The Microplastics Crisis

We, as humans, ingest around 5 grams of plastic a week. Here's what that means — and what we can do about it.

What Are Microplastics?

Microplastics, MPs for short, are basically synthetic polymer particles smaller than 5mm in diameter. There are two types of MPs: primary and secondary.

Primary Microplastics

Primary MPs are MPs intentionally produced at a small size (like microbeads in skincare).

Secondary Microplastics

Secondary MPs are the result of environmental fragmentation and weathering of larger plastic products (like plastic bags, bottles and fishing gear).

Impact on Marine Life

There are many impacts of MPs on marine life. There is widespread contamination of MPs in the ocean which leads to unintentional ingestion in aquatic animals. Marine fish are consistent consumers of microplastics, mistaking them for prey or ingesting them indirectly. Fish from areas with high human coastal population density and poor waste management (like the Asia-Pacific region) showed a higher prevalence and concentration of MPs. This points to land-based sources as a primary contributor.

MPs accumulating in fish and other smaller marine life could lead to the harmful process of trophic transfer resulting in toxic bioaccumulation. This could lead to the endangerment and extinction of many marine species. These are indirect dangers to marine life.

The direct dangers of MPs are when the plastics are already inside the body of the marine life (this is similar to MP impacts on humans). MP poisoning can cause obstructive damage and internal abrasions in the gastrointestinal tract. Furthermore, the accumulation of non-nutritive plastic can create a sense of false satiety, reducing feeding and leading to energetic depletion and weight loss. The presence of foreign synthetic particles in the gut can trigger chronic inflammatory responses and suppress the immune system, making fish more susceptible to disease.

Impact on Human Health

MPs are toxic, as they are not pure polymers, they can contain chemical additives (like phthalates, flame retardants and stabilizers) which can deeply affect the body on a physiological level. They can cause extreme damage to us through acting as a vessel for harmful pathogens. The “Trojan Horse” effect states that MPs act as a vector for pathogens (Vibrio species). This could lead to greater damage to human health than the effect of the MP particle/contaminant alone.

How Refyber Helps

Our organisation helps by preventing the microfibers released from clothing in the dryer to enter the ocean. In a usual household, there are filters that prevent the microplastics from entering the water stream however, these microplastics end up getting thrown into the trash which will ultimately end up in the ocean from landfill. Therefore, Refyber focuses on reducing this by repurposing the microplastics filtered out by the dryers and turning them into eco-paper. This allows a break from the cycle of harm and instead provides opportunity for education and awareness, whilst making a positive physical impact as well.

Collect

We gather the microfibers filtered out by household dryers — fibres that would otherwise be thrown away and end up in the ocean via landfill.

Repurpose

The collected microplastics are turned into eco-paper — breaking the cycle of harm.

Educate

Eco-paper opens the door to education and awareness, whilst making a positive physical impact as well.
